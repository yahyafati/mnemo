Photosynthesis explained
Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae and some bacteria turn light energy into chemical energy stored in sugar. It takes place mostly in the leaves, inside organelles called chloroplasts.
The overall reaction needs carbon dioxide, water and light, and produces glucose and oxygen. The light-dependent reactions happen in the thylakoid membranes, where chlorophyll absorbs mostly red and blue light.
The Calvin cycle, which does not need light directly, uses the energy carriers made in the first stage to fix carbon dioxide into sugar in the stroma.
