How tides work
Tides are the regular rise and fall of the sea. They are caused mainly by the pull of the moon, and, to a lesser degree, by the pull of the sun.
The moon pulls hardest on the side of the Earth that faces it, which makes the water bulge towards it, and a second bulge forms on the far side, so most coasts see two high tides a day.
When the sun and moon line up, at new and full moon, their pulls add together and the tides are larger. These are called spring tides, although they have nothing to do with the season.
