# Cellular respiration

Cellular respiration is the set of reactions by which cells break down glucose and other fuel molecules to make
**ATP** (adenosine triphosphate), the molecule that carries energy for most cellular work. In eukaryotes it takes
place partly in the cytoplasm and partly in the **mitochondria**. The overall reaction is
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O, and it releases about 2,870 kJ per mole of glucose.

## Glycolysis

**Glycolysis** takes place in the cytoplasm and does not need oxygen. It splits one molecule of glucose (six
carbons) into two molecules of **pyruvate** (three carbons each). The pathway has ten steps, catalysed by ten
enzymes. Its first half uses two ATP; its second half makes four ATP and two **NADH**, so the net yield is **2 ATP
and 2 NADH per glucose**. The step catalysed by **phosphofructokinase** is the main control point: ATP inhibits
the enzyme and AMP activates it.

## Pyruvate oxidation

When oxygen is present, pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix. There the **pyruvate dehydrogenase complex**
removes one carbon as CO₂ and attaches the remaining two-carbon acetyl group to coenzyme A, forming **acetyl-CoA**.
Each pyruvate also yields one NADH.

## The citric acid cycle

The **citric acid cycle** (Krebs cycle) runs in the mitochondrial matrix. Acetyl-CoA joins **oxaloacetate** (four
carbons) to form **citrate** (six carbons). Over eight steps two carbons leave as CO₂ and oxaloacetate is
regenerated. Each turn produces **3 NADH, 1 FADH₂ and 1 GTP** (or ATP). Because each glucose gives two acetyl-CoA,
the cycle turns twice per glucose.

## Oxidative phosphorylation

The **electron transport chain** sits in the inner mitochondrial membrane. NADH and FADH₂ hand their electrons to a
series of protein complexes (I to IV); **oxygen is the final electron acceptor** and is reduced to water. As
electrons pass along the chain, complexes I, III and IV pump protons from the matrix into the intermembrane space,
building a **proton gradient**. Protons flow back through **ATP synthase**, which uses the flow to make ATP from ADP
and phosphate. This coupling of the gradient to ATP synthesis is **chemiosmosis**, proposed by Peter Mitchell in 1961.

Each NADH yields about **2.5 ATP** and each FADH₂ about **1.5 ATP**. In total, one glucose gives roughly **30 to 32
ATP** under aerobic conditions.

## Fermentation

Without oxygen the electron transport chain stops, and NADH can no longer be reoxidised there. Cells then regenerate
NAD⁺ by **fermentation**, so that glycolysis can continue. In **lactic acid fermentation** (muscle cells, some
bacteria) pyruvate is reduced to lactate. In **alcoholic fermentation** (yeast) pyruvate is converted to ethanol and
CO₂. Fermentation yields only the 2 ATP of glycolysis per glucose.
