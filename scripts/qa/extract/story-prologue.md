# Prologue

The ferry to Vaasa left at six in the evening, and by half past five the queue of cars already reached back past the
petrol station. Ilse sat in the passenger seat with the map open on her knees, although there was nothing left to
navigate: the road ended at the water, and the water was the only way on.

Her brother had fallen asleep against the window. He was eleven that summer and had decided, somewhere north of
Turku, that he was too old to be interested in boats. She envied him the sleep. She had been awake since three,
when their mother had started loading the car in the dark, moving quickly and saying nothing, the way she did when
she had already made a decision and did not want to be asked about it.

The gulls were loud over the harbour. A man in an orange jacket walked down the line of cars, bending to each window
and saying something that made the drivers nod. When he reached them he looked at the three of them, at the boxes on
the back seat and the bicycle tied to the roof, and he smiled at Ilse as if he knew exactly where they were going and
why. He told their mother they would be on board in twenty minutes.

They were on board in forty. Ilse stood at the rail as the town slid away, its roofs going grey and then blue, and
then there was only the long low line of the coast and the sound of the engines under her feet. Her mother came and
stood beside her and, for the first time that day, put an arm around her shoulders.

"We're not coming back, are we," Ilse said.

Her mother did not answer, and after a while Ilse stopped waiting for her to. The coast had gone. Ahead there was
nothing yet, only the evening light on the water, and the ferry pushing on into it as if it knew the way.

It was three years before Ilse understood what her mother had been running from, and twenty before she went back to
see the house. This book is about those twenty years.
